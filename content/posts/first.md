Hi, I'm Kento "sakanate" Maruyama. This is my first time writing a blog. So, I'll write about why I started this blog, a bit about me, and my projects.

## Why Blogging?

It seems like I can't get jobs without showing that I'm a good developer. This blog is also a diary and a way to practice English.
I hope my posts help someone out there.

## "sakanate"

I was born in Japan and started programming on a laptop my grandpa gave me when I was in junior high.
Java was the first, and then [*Bloody Monday*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloody_Monday_(TV_series)), a Japanese TV drama about hackers, led me to Python and C.
In my college dormitory, we played CS:GO and Minecraft over our local network. Every night was a LAN party.

I sold my soul to computers and have spent over a decade in front of screens. That's how I spent my youth!
"sakanate" is the handle I often use in games. It's a mix of the band [sakanaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakanaction) and N's name from [*Death Note*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Note).
It's my favorite username because it's always unique and available.

## Projects

### indiff.io

I'm developing a search engine called indiff.io that takes your problem as a search query and returns web pages with solutions. Writing a crawler from scratch in Rust gives me plenty of new discoveries. I'd always wanted to build something in Rust, but never actually started. Now I'm enjoying its difficulty and making slow progress day by day.

### Contributing to Servo

I've decided to start contributing to [Servo](https://github.com/servo/servo) on weekends. I felt like I needed a sensei to learn Rust. Servo has a huge, mature codebase, so I thought it would be a great place to learn how to write Rust code. I'm also eager to understand how browser engines work. I haven't made any contributions yet. Still learning the concepts and main crates.

I've been looking at issue [#48890](https://github.com/servo/servo/issues/48890) recently, and reading through some of the blob implementation. The issue is a simple replacement task for beginners, but I was impressed by how well-documented it is. That's really professional work!

### Programming Workshop

Lastly, let me tell you about a programming workshop I held at the community center near my house. The workshop was open to all ages and limited to four people. Using desktop PCs running Kubuntu, each participant launched their own Apache web server and wrote some HTML. Surprisingly, people in their 70s and 80s signed up, and they ran `sudo systemctl start apache2` in the terminal. It was so rare and fun to see seniors trying their hand at typing commands like that!

The leader of our neighborhood association also joined us. He had never typed a command before and didn't even know where the `/` key was, but he still finished his own "About Me" page in HTML. We wrapped up by visiting each other's pages. Most of it went over their heads, but I think they got a little taste of the developer's world. I'm planning another workshop sometime soon!
